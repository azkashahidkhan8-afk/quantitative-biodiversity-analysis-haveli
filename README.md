# Quantitative Biodiversity Analysis in Haveli District, AJK, Pakistan

## 1. Research Background

Biodiversity assessment is important for understanding species composition, distribution patterns and ecological variation across different habitats. Differences in vegetation, land use and habitat characteristics can influence the occurrence and distribution of species.

This undergraduate research project examined biodiversity patterns across different habitat types in Haveli District, Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK), Pakistan. The study used field observations and quantitative biodiversity indices to document species diversity and compare species composition among habitats.

## 2. Research Objectives

The main objectives of this study were to:

- Document species diversity and composition across selected habitats in Haveli District.
- Record species occurrence and the number of individuals observed in different habitat types.
- Quantitatively assess biodiversity using diversity and evenness metrics.
- Compare species composition and similarity among habitats.
- Examine patterns of species distribution across the surveyed habitats.

## 3. Study Location and Research Period

- **Study area:** Haveli District, Azad Jammu and Kashmir (AJK), Pakistan.
- **Research period:** September 2022 – May 2023.
- **Study focus:** Assessment of species diversity and distribution across 10 habitat categories.

## 4. Field Survey Methodology

The study employed the **point-count method** for field observations and species recording.

Field data collection involved recording species observations and counting individuals at survey points across the selected habitat types. The collected observations were organized to examine species occurrence, abundance and distribution patterns within the study area.

## 5. Biodiversity Records

The field survey documented:

| Parameter | Recorded total |
|---|---:|
| Individuals | 493 |
| Species | 58 |
| Families | 32 |
| Habitat categories | 10 |

These records provided the dataset for subsequent quantitative biodiversity and habitat-similarity analyses.

## 6. Habitat Categories

The survey covered 10 habitat categories representing different environmental and land-use conditions.

Examples of the habitats included:

- Agricultural areas
- Plantations
- Rangeland
- Settlements
- Sub-alpine habitats
- Sub-tropical Chir Pine forests
- Waterbodies

The habitat categories were used to examine variation in species occurrence and composition across the study area.

## 7. Quantitative Data Analysis

Biodiversity and habitat similarity were evaluated using the following indices:

### Diversity and Evenness

- **Shannon–Wiener Diversity Index (H′):** Used to quantify species diversity.
- **Simpson's Diversity Index (D):** Used to assess diversity and species dominance.
- **Species Evenness:** Used to examine the distribution of individuals among recorded species.

### Habitat Similarity

- **Sørensen Similarity Index:** Used to compare species composition between habitats.
- **Jaccard Similarity Index:** Used to assess species overlap between habitats.

Microsoft Excel was used for data organization, tabulation, quantitative analysis and graphical presentation of the findings.

## 8. Key Findings

The survey recorded 493 individuals belonging to 58 species and 32 families across 10 habitat categories.

### Biodiversity Indices

The quantitative analysis produced the following results:

- **Shannon–Wiener Diversity Index (H′):** 3.81
- **Simpson’s Diversity Index (D):** 0.973
- **Species Evenness:** 0.212

These indices were used to characterize species diversity, dominance and the distribution of individuals among recorded species in the study area.

### Habitat Similarity Analysis

Habitat comparisons using Sørensen and Jaccard similarity indices revealed the following patterns:

**Sørensen Similarity Index**
- Highest similarity: Agricultural Land and Sub-Tropical Chir Pine Forest (0.580).
- Lowest similarity: Agricultural Land and Waterbody (0.333).

**Jaccard Similarity Index**
- Highest similarity: Agricultural Land and Sub-Tropical Chir Pine Forest (0.409).
- Lowest similarity: Waterbody and Plantation (0.140).

Both similarity indices identified Agricultural Land and Sub-Tropical Chir Pine Forest as the most similar habitat pair in terms of recorded species composition.

## 9. Research Limitations

The findings should be interpreted within the scope of the surveyed locations, habitat categories and research period (September 2022–May 2023). Point-count observations may also be influenced by species detectability and field-survey conditions.

The results represent the biodiversity patterns documented during the study period and should not necessarily be interpreted as a complete inventory of all species present throughout Haveli District.
